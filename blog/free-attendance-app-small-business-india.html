Free attendance app for a small business: what "free" should include

Free attendance apps are easy to find and hard to keep. Here is the difference between a free tier and a trial with the price hidden.

Published 7 September 2026 · Updated 11 September 2026 · Merik team · 4 min read

A free attendance app is genuinely free for a small business only if it handles your whole team without a user cap, captures a timestamp and location at check-in, connects leave to attendance, gives you a path to payroll, and lets you export everything — indefinitely. Most "free" tiers fail at least two of those: a five-user limit, thirty days of history, no export, or a check-in that works but a register that is paid. The list below is what to check before your team builds a habit on something that will start invoicing you at employee number six.

Key takeaways
  • The first check is the user cap. Free for five users is a trial for a fifteen-person business.
  • The second is history. Attendance is a statutory record; thirty days of retention on the free tier means the record is elsewhere or nowhere.
  • A check-in without timestamp and location is a WhatsApp message with a nicer button.
  • If leave lives in a different tool, or payroll needs an upload, the app is free and the month-end is not.
  • Ask what happens to your data if you stop. Export is the feature that keeps free honest.

Eight things free tiers usually cap

  1. Users — 5, 10 or 15. Check the number against your headcount in a year, not today.
  2. History — 30 or 90 days visible, the rest paid. Attendance registers should be kept for years.
  3. Location — check-in free, geolocation paid. For field staff, that is the feature.
  4. Reports — the monthly register, the one thing you need for payroll, behind the upgrade.
  5. Leave — attendance free, leave a separate module. Leave that is not linked marks people absent on approved holidays.
  6. Export — the data is yours to look at and not to take.
  7. Admins — one admin login on free, so the owner is the only one who can approve anything.
  8. Support and setup — fine to charge for; just know before you need it.

What a working free tier has to include

  • Unlimited employees, with employees signing themselves up so onboarding costs the admin nothing. The onboarding checklist.
  • Check-in and check-out with a server timestamp and location, reverse-geocoded to a readable place — see how the capture methods compare.
  • Late marks and half-days by configurable rules, not judged per cell.
  • Leave and WFH as requests that update the attendance record when approved.
  • A monthly register per employee, and a path from it to payroll that does not involve re-typing.
  • Employee self-service — their own history, their own balances, their own payslip.
  • Export of the record, and a stated position on what happens if you leave.
  • Security basics — tenant isolation, role-based access, a stated data location. The eight questions.

Five questions to ask before your team builds a habit

  1. How many employees can use it free, and does that number change?
  2. How long is attendance history kept on the free tier?
  3. Is location captured at check-in, and stored as a place name?
  4. Do approved leave and WFH update attendance automatically?
  5. Can I export every record as CSV today, and what happens to the data if I stop?

A vendor that answers all five plainly is selling a free tier. One that answers three is selling a trial. Either is fine, as long as you know which one you are installing on thirty phones.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a genuinely free attendance app for small businesses in India?

Yes, but check the shape of the free tier rather than the word. A genuinely free tier has no user cap you will hit, keeps history indefinitely, captures timestamp and location at check-in, links leave to attendance, and lets you export. Many free apps cap users at five or ten or keep only thirty days of history, which makes them trials.

What should a free attendance app include?

Unlimited employees with self sign-up, check-in and check-out with a server timestamp and a reverse-geocoded location, late marks and half-days computed by configurable rules, leave and work-from-home requests that update attendance when approved, a monthly register per employee that feeds payroll without re-typing, employee self-service, CSV export and basic tenant security.

Do free attendance apps work for field employees?

Only if location capture is included on the free tier. Check-in with a stored, reverse-geocoded location is what makes a field employee's attendance verifiable; some apps put that behind a paid plan. Also check that location is captured at the punch only, not tracked continuously.

Can a free attendance app connect to payroll?

Some can, if attendance and payroll are modules of the same workspace and share one record. If the app only produces a register that you upload or re-type into a separate payroll tool, the app is free but the monthly reconciliation is not — and that reconciliation is where most small-business payroll errors come from.

What are the hidden costs of free attendance software?

Per-user pricing that begins at a headcount you will reach, a separate paid tool for leave or payroll, biometric hardware the app assumes, the absence of export so leaving means re-typing history, and the owner's own time re-keying the register into a salary sheet every month.
