Gratuity calculation in India: formula, eligibility, examples

Gratuity is the exit payment most small businesses forget to provision for and then discover at the worst moment. The arithmetic is simple; the record-keeping is what goes wrong.

Published 4 September 2026 · Updated 11 September 2026 · Merik team · 5 min read

Gratuity in India is calculated as 15 days' wages for every completed year of service, using the formula: last drawn wages × 15 ÷ 26 × years of service. "Wages" means basic pay plus dearness allowance; 26 represents working days in a month; and service beyond six months in the final year counts as a full year. It is payable by establishments with 10 or more employees to anyone who leaves after five years of continuous service — or, since the labour codes took effect in November 2025, after one year for fixed-term employees, pro rata. Gratuity received is tax-free up to ₹20 lakh. Facts checked 11 September 2026.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: last drawn basic + DA × 15 ÷ 26 × completed years. On ₹30,000 wages and 7 years that is ₹1,21,154.
  • Eligibility: five years' continuous service for regular employees; one year, pro rata, for fixed-term employees under the Code on Social Security.
  • The final year rounds up if it exceeds six months — 7 years 7 months counts as 8.
  • The 50% wages rule raises the gratuity base for anyone whose basic was set low. Recalculate your liability.
  • Gratuity is due within 30 days of it becoming payable. Provision monthly — roughly 4.81% of basic — so it is never a surprise in the full and final settlement.

The formula and what each term means

Gratuity = last drawn wages × 15 ÷ 26 × years of service

  • Last drawn wages — basic pay plus dearness allowance in the last month of service. Not gross, not CTC. Under the labour codes, "wages" also includes any excluded allowances above the 50% line.
  • 15 ÷ 26 — fifteen days' pay out of a 26-working-day month. The fraction is fixed by the statute for establishments covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act (now the Code on Social Security).
  • Years of service — completed years, with the final partial year rounded up if it exceeds six months.

For employers not covered by the Act (fewer than 10 employees) who nonetheless pay gratuity, a half-month's average salary per year on a 30-day basis is the usual convention, but it is contractual rather than statutory.

Three worked examples

Example 1Basic + DA ₹30,000; service 7 years 2 months → 7 years. 30,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 7 = ₹1,21,154.
Example 2Basic + DA ₹30,000; service 7 years 8 months → rounds to 8 years. 30,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 8 = ₹1,38,462. Six months and one day is worth a full year's gratuity — employees know this; your provisioning should too.
Example 3 (fixed-term)Basic + DA ₹25,000; 18-month fixed-term contract completed. Pro rata: 25,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 1.5 = ₹21,635. Before November 2025 this employee received nothing.

Eligibility: the five-year rule and its edges

  • Five years of continuous service is the standard qualifying period for regular employees. Continuous service is not broken by authorised leave, maternity leave, sickness, or lay-off.
  • Four years and 240 days. Several High Courts have held that an employee who has worked 240 days in the fifth year has completed five years for gratuity purposes. The position is not uniform nationally; a cautious employer pays.
  • Death or disablement — the five-year requirement is waived.
  • Fixed-term employees — one year of continuous service, pro rata, under the Code on Social Security.
  • Forfeiture is possible only for termination on grounds of wilful misconduct causing damage, or riotous conduct — and only to the extent of the damage, or wholly for moral turpitude. Resignation never forfeits gratuity.

Tax treatment and timing

Gratuity received by an employee is exempt from income tax up to ₹20 lakh across their working life; amounts above that are taxable as salary. The employer must pay within 30 days of the amount becoming due — the date of leaving — and interest applies to late payment. In practice gratuity is settled with the full and final settlement, which makes the exit date the deadline.

Provisioning: how not to be surprised

Gratuity accrues at 15 ÷ 26 of a month's wages per year — 4.81% of basic plus DA. A company with ten employees on an average ₹25,000 basic accrues roughly ₹12,000 a month in future gratuity. Over five years that is ₹7.2 lakh that will be paid out as people leave, whether or not it was ever booked.

Two things make it manageable. First, carry a monthly provision in the accounts at 4.81% of basic for every employee, from their joining date. Second, keep the inputs where they cannot drift: joining date, every salary revision with its effective date, and the exit date. The calculation is one line; the argument is always about the inputs. Frequently asked questions

How is gratuity calculated in India?

Gratuity equals last drawn basic pay plus dearness allowance, multiplied by 15, divided by 26, multiplied by completed years of service. Service in the final year counts as a full year if it exceeds six months. On wages of ₹30,000 and seven years of service the gratuity is ₹1,21,154.

Is gratuity payable after 4 years and 240 days?

Several High Court decisions treat 240 days worked in the fifth year as completing five years of service for gratuity, but the position is not uniform across India. Many employers pay on that basis to avoid a dispute; if you intend not to, take advice for your state before refusing.

Are fixed-term employees eligible for gratuity?

Yes. Under the Code on Social Security, which came into force on 21 November 2025, a fixed-term employee who completes one year of continuous service is entitled to gratuity on a pro-rata basis, rather than needing five years.

Is gratuity calculated on basic salary or gross salary?

On basic pay plus dearness allowance — the statutory definition of wages — not on gross or CTC. Under the labour codes, if excluded allowances exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back into wages, which increases the gratuity base for employees whose basic was set low.

Is gratuity taxable?

Gratuity received is exempt from income tax up to a lifetime limit of ₹20 lakh. Any amount above that is taxed as salary income in the year received.

When must gratuity be paid after resignation?

Within 30 days of the date it becomes payable, which is the employee's last working day. Late payment attracts simple interest. In practice it is settled with the full and final settlement, so the exit date is the deadline.
